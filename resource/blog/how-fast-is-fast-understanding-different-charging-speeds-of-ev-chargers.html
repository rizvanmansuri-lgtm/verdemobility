Article 1 : How Fast is Fast? Understanding different charging speeds of EV chargers

Electric Vehicles are here to stay, and their revolution is on full swing. As EVs are becoming more common, one of the most important considerations for drivers is charging speed. Unlike traditional refueling methods that barely take minutes, EV charging time varies depending on the type of charger used. There are three primary charging levels – Level 1(Home chargers) , Level 2 (AC Chargers) and Level 3(DC Chargers).

Level-1 Charging is slow and steady and is the most basic form of EV Charging. It involves plugging the vehicle into a standard 120 volt household outlet using charging cable provided by most manufacturers. While this method is convenient and requires no special installation, it is the slowest charging option. Verde Mobility offers Ksipra Compact AC, a compact home charger that is convenient for overnight charging at home, low-mileage drivers or emergency use but is quite impractical for frequent long-distance driving.

Level- 2 charging is significantly faster than Level-1 and is a preferred option for personal and public charging stations. This type of chargers operates on power supply similar to household appliances. Installing a Level 2 charger typically requires professional installation. Verde Mobility offers a range of 3.3kW to 44kW in AC chargers. It is best for home, work and public charging stations. Many businesses, shopping centers and parking garages now offer Level 2 chargers, making it easy for EV owners to recharge while running errands. This charging level balances speed and cost, making it a popular choice for most EV drivers.

Level 3 charging or DC charging is for those who need a quick on the go charge. Unlike level 1 and level 2 chargers which provide AC power, the car's onboard charger is converted into DC power. But here DC fast chargers supply DC power directly to the battery, significantly reducing charging time. Verde Mobility offers a range of 30kW to 240kW in DC chargers or fast chargers, these are best for road trips, highway rest stops and emergency fast charging. DC Chargers prove to be the most effective option for heavy commercial EV and small EVs for quick charge. Although they have a few limitations like higher costs, potential battery degradation with frequent use and the fact that not all EVs support DC fast charging. DC Fast Chargers are commonly found along highways, enabling long-distance EV travel by reducing charging downtime.

Choosing the right EV charging speed depends on driving habits, budget and access to charging stations. As EV technology advances, charging speeds are expected to improve, making electric vehicle ownership more convenient and accessible.
